Methods

AP model
O'Hara-Rudy 2011 undiseased human ventricular model (41-variable ODE system). Integrated with LSODA via scipy.integrate.odeint, 50 paced beats at 1000 ms CL, dt = 0.5 ms.
hERG block
Competitive binding: blocki = Cfree / (Cfree + IC50). Combined block: 1 − ∏(1 − blocki). Applied as GKr scaling: GKreff = GKrmax × (1 − total block).
Sympathomimetic
Stimulants (MPH, AMP) modeled as 10% CL reduction (HR increase proxy) + 15% GCaL upregulation. Applied to each stimulant in combination independently.
Autonomic
Alpha-2 agonists (CLO, GUA) modeled as 10% CL increase (bradycardia) + 5% GNa reduction (conduction slowing). Produces negative ΔQTc via Bazett correction.
QTc
APD90 extracted from last beat V-trace as time from upstroke to 90% repolarization. QTc = APD90 / √(RRs) (Bazett). ΔQTc = QTcdrug − QTcbaseline.
IC50 sources
Electrophysiology (patch clamp) values preferred. Sources: ChEMBL v37, Witchel 2002, Redfern 2003, Kongsamut 2002, Polak 2009, Kramer 2013, Perrin 2008. Quality A (patch clamp): IMI, NOR, RIS, QUE, ARI, SER, FLU. Quality B/C: ESC, CLO, GUA, MPH, AMP.
Free Cmax
Therapeutic free plasma Cmax derived from published adult pharmacokinetic data and protein binding fractions. All values adult-derived; see limitations.
05

Limitations & assumptions

  • All free Cmax values are adult-derived. Adolescent pharmacokinetics differ substantially due to developmental CYP2D6 expression variability, which metabolizes risperidone, aripiprazole, fluoxetine, nortriptyline, and imipramine. Cmax may shift 1.5–3× in pediatric populations.
  • The ORd model represents the undiseased adult human ventricular action potential. Adolescent baseline QTc is shorter by approximately 10–15 ms, and autonomic tone differs developmentally. Model has not been validated against pediatric ECG data.
  • Sympathomimetic and autonomic modifiers (10% CL shift) are phenomenological approximations. Dose-dependent beta-adrenergic signaling cascades (PKA-mediated ICaL/IKs upregulation) are not fully mechanistically modeled.
  • Active metabolite contributions not included except where noted. Norfluoxetine (fluoxetine metabolite, IC50 ~3000 nM) and 9-OH-risperidone may contribute additional hERG block in vivo.
  • Independent binding site assumption for combined IKr block. If drugs compete for the same hERG binding site, combined block will be overestimated.
  • Guanfacine, methylphenidate, and amphetamine IC50 values are estimates (data quality C). Stimulant cardiac risk characterization through sympathomimetic pathway is mechanistically appropriate but quantitatively uncertain.
  • Pubertal hormonal modulation of IKs (estrogen increases IKs, testosterone decreases it) is not modeled. Sex differences in adolescent QTc are clinically significant and represent a key limitation for individual-level risk prediction.
  • 50-beat simulations may not represent true electrophysiological steady state. Full convergence typically requires 200+ beats; ΔQTc estimates carry approximately ±3–5 ms uncertainty from this source.

Pharmacovigilance validation — FDA FAERS

Model predictions were externally validated against FDA FAERS adverse event reports (2015Q1–2024Q4). Pediatric cases (age < 18) were filtered from 16.1 million total reports, yielding 552,832 pediatric cases. Reporting odds ratios (ROR) were computed for primary cardiac preferred terms (QT prolonged, Torsade de pointes, ventricular arrhythmia, cardiac arrest, sudden cardiac death) in pediatric cases co-reported with each drug and drug combination.

Primary validation

MPH+SER ROR 12.8

n=578 pediatric co-reports with tight CI. One of the most common adolescent polypharmacy combinations (ADHD + depression/anxiety) correctly flagged as MODERATE risk by the model via sympathomimetic mechanism.

Guanfacine discordance

Model vs. FAERS gap

ARI+GUA (ROR 10.25) and QUE+GUA (ROR 13.15) show strong FAERS signal despite low model ΔQTc. Model lacks a PR/AV conduction pathway. Guanfacine risk in polypharmacy is likely conduction-mediated, not QTc-mediated.

CYP2D6 interaction gap

PK not modeled

QUE+FLU (ROR 9.18) and ARI+SER (ROR 6.78) show FAERS signal despite low model risk. Fluoxetine inhibits CYP2D6, raising quetiapine and aripiprazole plasma levels 2–4×. Model uses fixed adult Cmax with no PK interaction term.

Sympathomimetic signal

Heart rate increased: 230

Second most common cardiac PT in high-risk drug reports after ECG QT prolonged (297). Directly validates the model's sympathomimetic pathway as a real cardiac risk mechanism, not captured by hERG screening alone.
